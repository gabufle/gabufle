### 🧪 About me

I spend my days at the bench: pipetting, running gels, growing cells, troubleshooting assays that refuse to cooperate. Wet lab science is deeply hands-on, and I love that about it. But I also know firsthand how much of a researcher's time gets swallowed by things that shouldn't require a human at all! manually tracking the literature, copy-pasting data between spreadsheets, repeating the same searches every week over and over and over again.

That frustration is what got me into programming. Not a computer science course, not a career pivot — just the desire to stop doing things a computer could do better.

My focus is on building tools **for bench scientists, by a bench scientist**. Practical software that slots into the real workflow of a wet lab: staying current with arXiv and PubMed, automating data wrangling, and reducing the cognitive overhead that doesn't contribute to the actual science. The goal isn't to replace the researcher — it's to give them their time back.


I believe the next wave of progress in life sciences won't come from choosing between bench work and computation — it'll come from people who are comfortable doing both.
